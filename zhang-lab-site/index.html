Louisiana State University • Department of Biological Sciences

Decoding how microbiomes regulate host physiology, aging, and resilience.

The Zhang Lab uses Caenorhabditis elegans to uncover how naturally associated microbes shape insulin signaling, life-history tradeoffs, gut health, and responses to environmental stress.

Explore Research Join the Lab

Research Themes

  • Microbiome control of host development, reproduction, and lifespan
  • Insulin/IGF-1 signaling in host–microbe interactions
  • Gut dysbiosis, aging, and intestinal barrier decline
  • Environmental stressors, oxidative damage, and microbial ecology
  • High-throughput imaging, multi-omics, and predictive modeling

Microbiome and aging

We investigate how defined microbial communities shape host life-history traits and identify bacterial functions that promote either reproductive success, lifespan extension, or both.

Host signaling pathways

Our work focuses on conserved endocrine pathways, especially insulin/IGF-1 signaling, to understand how hosts interpret microbial cues across tissues and across the lifespan.

Tools and resources

We combine genetics, fluorescent reporters, imaging, microbiome resources, and computational analysis to build reproducible datasets and community-facing resources.

Our approach

We integrate worm genetics, defined microbiome communities, microscopy, functional genomics, metabolomics, and machine learning to discover principles of host–microbe interaction that can generalize across animals.

Current projects span insulin-like peptide regulation, microbiome selection, internal hatching, age-associated dysbiosis, and the biological consequences of environmentally persistent free radicals.
